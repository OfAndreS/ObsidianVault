Present continuous

The present continuous is the verb tense that refers to an ongoing action. 

This verbs are present continuous: 

| Subject         | Auxiliary verb | Subject + A. verb        | Verb    |
| --------------- | -------------- | ------------------------ | ------- |
| i               | am             | i'm                      | driving |
| he/she/it       | is             | he's / she's / it's      | working |
| we / you / they | are            | we're / you're / they're | doing   |

Affirmative: 
- Subject + am/is/are + verb with -ing. 
- I am working.

Negative: 
- Subject + am/is/are + not + verb with -ing.
- She is not sleeping.

Interrogative: 
- Am/is/are + subject + verb with -ing. 
- Are they studying?

---

Some examples 

I'm doing something.
- I start doing it and i haven't finished; I'm in the middle of doing it.

I'm reading a really good book at the moment. 
- I say "i'm reading" but i'm not reading at the time of speaking.
- I mean that i start reading the book, but i not finished it yet. I'm in the middle of reading it.

Comum verbs

getting, becoming, changing, improving, starting, beginning, increasing, rising, falling, growing

- Is your English getting better? (not Does your English get better)
- The population of the world is increasing very fast. (not increases)
- At first I didn’t like my job, but I’m starting to enjoy it now. (not I start)

The present simple is the verb tense that refers to things in general or to say that something happens all the time.

### **Affirmative Sentences: I, We, You, They**

|**Subject**|**Main Verb**|**Complement**|
|---|---|---|
|I|do|my homework.|
|We|work|very hard.|
|You|drive|fast.|
|They|dance|well.|

### **Affirmative Sentences: He, She, It**

|**Subject**|**Main Verb**|**Complement**|
|---|---|---|
|He|does|his best.|
|She|works|at a hospital.|
|It|opens|at 8 AM.|

### **Rules**

For the third-person singular (he, she, it), when the verb ends with the letters o, s, x, z, ch, or sh, it is necessary to conjugate the verb with -es in affirmative sentences.

### **Negative Sentences**

|**Subject**|**Auxiliary Verb**|**Main Verb**|**Complement**|
|---|---|---|---|
|I / we / you / they|don't|live|in London.|
|he / she / it|doesn't|watch|TV.|

### **Interrogative Sentences**

|**Auxiliary Verb**|**Subject**|**Main Verb**|**Complement**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Do|I / we / you / they|go|out tonight?|
|Does|he / she / it|drive|to work?|

### **Important Notes**

- **Regras de escrita:** Para he, she e it, a maioria dos verbos recebe apenas a letra s. Verbos terminados nas letras o, sh, ch, ss ou x recebem as letras es (o verbo go vira goes). Verbos terminados em consoante mais a letra y perdem o y e recebem as letras ies (o verbo study vira studies).
    
- **A Grande Exceção:** O verbo have (ter) é irregular. Ele não segue a regra de adicionar a letra s. Nas frases afirmativas para he, she e it, a palavra have muda completamente para has.
    
- **O Verbo Ser ou Estar:** As palavras am, is e are possuem regras próprias. Elas são independentes e nunca se misturam com os verbos auxiliares do e does nas frases.
    
- **Palavras de Frequência:** O uso de palavras que indicam rotina é muito comum nesse tempo verbal. Palavras como always e never costumam aparecer logo antes da ação principal.
